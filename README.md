# Aletheia

Aletheia is a personal life analysis app that I built to better understand myself through data.

The project started as three separate apps:

* Checkpoints — a task, habit, and long-term goal tracker.
* BellyLog — a digestive health and lifestyle tracker.
* Palimora — a journal and life-pattern analysis system.

Over time, I realized that these parts of life are connected. My productivity affects my stress. My stress affects my stomach. My physical health affects my mood. My mood affects my motivation.

Instead of treating these areas separately, Aletheia attempts to view them as parts of the same system.

The name "Aletheia" comes from the ancient Greek concept of truth, disclosure, or uncovering what was previously hidden. That idea fits the goal of the project: helping reveal patterns that are difficult to notice when living day to day.

---

## Why I Built This

For several years I have dealt with recurring digestive issues and an IBS-D diagnosis. At the same time, I have also been interested in personal growth, journaling, productivity systems, and understanding how habits shape everyday life.

I found myself asking questions such as:

* Why are some weeks better than others?
* Do certain foods consistently affect my stomach?
* Does poor sleep influence my mood and productivity?
* Why do I feel more motivated during some periods of life?
* Are there patterns hidden inside my daily routines?

I wanted a system that could help answer these questions using my own data rather than relying purely on memory.

Aletheia is my attempt to build that system.

---

## Core Idea

Aletheia is based on a simple assumption:

People are complex systems.

Our actions, emotions, physical health, environment, relationships, ambitions, and habits constantly influence one another.

Rather than treating these areas as isolated categories, Aletheia tries to identify connections between them.

The goal is not to tell someone what to do.

The goal is to help them see themselves more clearly.

---

## Modules

### Checkpoints

Checkpoints focuses on action and progress.

It tracks:

* Daily tasks
* Long-term goals
* Habit consistency
* Completion patterns
* Productivity trends

This module answers questions such as:

* What am I consistently following through on?
* What goals keep getting postponed?
* When am I most productive?
* What habits seem sustainable over time?

---

### BellyLog

BellyLog focuses on digestive health and lifestyle factors.

It tracks:

* Meals
* Symptoms
* Bowel movements
* Sleep
* Stress
* Daily health check-ins

The purpose is not to diagnose medical conditions.

Instead, it helps identify possible relationships between lifestyle choices and digestive symptoms.

Questions it may help explore:

* Do certain foods appear before symptom flare-ups?
* Does stress influence digestive health?
* Does sleep quality affect symptoms?
* Are there recurring weekly patterns?

---

### Palimora

Palimora focuses on reflection and emotional patterns.

It tracks:

* Journals
* Mood logs
* Life pillar ratings
* Significant experiences
* Personal observations

The goal is to create a record of everyday life that can later be analyzed for patterns.

Questions it may help explore:

* What contributes to good weeks?
* What consistently drains energy?
* What themes keep appearing in journals?
* Which areas of life seem neglected?
* What experiences bring genuine satisfaction?

---

## AI Integration

Aletheia uses AI as an analysis tool rather than a chatbot.

The AI is not intended to replace doctors, therapists, mentors, or life coaches.

Instead, it receives structured information from the different modules and attempts to identify patterns, trends, and relationships.

Examples include:

* Sleep quality and productivity
* Stress and digestive symptoms
* Mood and goal completion
* Journal themes and life satisfaction
* Behavioral patterns across multiple areas of life

The emphasis is on observation rather than advice.

---

## Weekly Analysis

One of the main features of Aletheia is its weekly analysis system.

Each week, information from all modules can be combined into a single report.

Rather than viewing productivity, health, and emotions separately, the analysis attempts to understand how they interact.

The purpose of these reports is not to provide answers.

The purpose is to provide perspective.

## Philosophy

Aletheia is not trying to optimize every minute of life.

It is not trying to gamify self-improvement.

It is not trying to turn a person into a productivity machine.

The project exists because memory is imperfect, emotions are complicated, and patterns are often difficult to see while living through them.

If there is a purpose behind Aletheia, it is simply this:

To help someone understand their own life a little more clearly than they could yesterday.
